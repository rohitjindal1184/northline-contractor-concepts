# Design System: Northline Build Co. — Contractor's Sample Book

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Contractor's Sample Book"**

This is a comparison gallery of finished directions, not a single theme with four skins. The dark gallery behaves like a contractor's project table: four complete expressions are laid out for comparison, Vertical Project Stories receives cinematic priority, and every card has one obvious action. Opening a concept replaces the table with a full visual world rather than carrying the gallery composition into the route.

The worlds share honest residential imagery, plainspoken copy, sharp controls, warm construction-derived accents, and a disciplined three-font vocabulary. Their composition stays deliberately different: Project Stories is immersive and cinematic; Daily Build Log is procedural and field-driven; Architect's Field Book is measured and tactile; Category Standard is familiar, open, and conversion-forward.

**Key Characteristics:**

- A dark comparison shell with warm paper card interiors and oversized editorial framing.
- Four route-specific palette and form systems that remain visibly independent.
- Large architectural photography used as evidence of craft, never as decorative wallpaper alone.
- Square controls, hairline rules, uppercase labels, and restrained warm accents.
- Plainspoken conversion copy with one clear estimate path inside every concept.

## Colors

Warm off-whites, near-black greens and navies, and fired-earth accents keep the whole sample book grounded in timber, paper, metal, and masonry without erasing the identity of each route.

### Primary

- **Shared Clay:** The gallery accent, shared form action, selection color, and Daily Build Log signal.
- **Stories Orange:** A brighter, more cinematic action color reserved for Project Stories.
- **Field Red:** A muted drafting-pencil red used for Field Book measurements, links, and actions.
- **Standard Orange:** A familiar construction orange used only in Category Standard's conversion hierarchy.

### Secondary

- **Field Sage:** The Field Book's quiet rule and diagram color; it should support the drafting system rather than compete with red.

### Neutral

- **Shared Forest and Shared Paper:** The gallery and Daily Build Log's core dark/light pair.
- **Stories Night and Stories Chalk:** A higher-contrast cinematic pair for image scrims, full-viewport stages, and the light summary surface.
- **Field Ink and Field Paper:** Blueprint ink on warm drafting stock, including the route's visible grid.
- **Standard Navy, Standard Stone, and White:** The cleanest and most conventional route palette, with navy carrying authority and stone separating sections.

### Named Rules

**The Four Palettes Rule.** Keep each route on its documented accent and neutral pair; do not normalize all four concepts to Shared Clay.

**The Warm Material Rule.** Use chalk, paper, stone, forest, navy, and fired-earth colors; avoid cold tech blues and synthetic gradients as primary surfaces.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Newsreader (with Georgia fallback)  
**Body Font:** DM Sans (with Arial fallback)  
**Label/Condensed Font:** Barlow Condensed (with Arial Narrow fallback)

**Character:** Newsreader gives project narratives the authority of an architectural monograph, DM Sans keeps homeowner guidance plain and legible, and Barlow Condensed turns numbers and field labels into compact construction notation. The Daily Build Log may promote the condensed face to hero scale; the other three worlds lead with the serif.

### Hierarchy

- **Editorial Display** (500, fluid 50–102px, 0.92 line-height): Gallery thesis, Project Stories headlines, Field Book headlines, and Category Standard headlines.
- **Condensed Display** (600, fluid 64–96px, 0.86 line-height): Daily Build Log's uppercase hero and estimate statements.
- **Title** (600, fluid 23–35px, 1 line-height): Concept-card and service-card titles.
- **Body** (400, 16px, 1.65 line-height): Explanations and conversion support; keep reading measures near 430–600px.
- **Label** (600, 12px, 0.08em tracking, uppercase): Buttons, navigation, steps, measurements, and project types.

### Named Rules

**The Two Voices Rule.** Serif tells the architectural story; condensed uppercase type names work, phases, and actions. DM Sans explains what happens next.

**The Tight Display Rule.** Large headings use compact line-height and modest negative tracking; never loosen them into generic marketing typography.

## Layout

The gallery uses a wide, edge-conscious grid: a three-part editorial intro followed by a two-column concept table, where the featured Project Stories card spans two rows. At 980px the table becomes one column and all major two-column content sections stack. At 680px outer gutters compress to 16–22px, navigation links collapse, form fields become a single column, and the floating back control moves from the lower-left to the upper-right below the route navigation.

Each route owns a distinct spatial model. Project Stories uses a full-viewport image stage with a floating right-side selector that becomes a three-column bottom rail on mobile. Daily Build Log uses an image hero followed by a four-phase horizontal rail that becomes a 2×2 grid. Field Book stays inside a visible 22px framed drafting sheet with an asymmetric hero; on mobile the frame narrows to 10px and the hero becomes a vertical sequence. Category Standard uses a familiar split hero and broad section bands, collapsing every grid into a direct single-column flow.

Desktop section breathing room is generally 100–130px, while mobile sections settle near 70–85px. Use responsive viewport gutters for route content, exact hairline alignment inside Field Book, and full-bleed imagery only where the current world calls for it.

### Named Rules

**The Complete World Rule.** A route may reuse shared primitives, but its composition must remain native to its concept; do not transplant the Field Book frame into Stories or the Stories rail into Standard.

**The One Clear Action Rule.** Every first viewport exposes one dominant action without surrounding it with competing buttons.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is flat by default. Depth comes from full-bleed photography, image scrims, tonal section changes, borders, and overlap. Shadows are rare and structural: the floating back control needs separation from any route, and the Field Book's inset project photograph lifts like a mounted print from the drafting surface.

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Floating Utility:** A soft downward shadow on the persistent back control so it survives photography and paper surfaces.
- **Mounted Print:** A low warm shadow beneath Field Book hero photography.
- **Inset Frame:** A one-pixel inner highlight on gallery media that keeps previews legible against the dark project table.

### Named Rules

**The Flat-by-Default Rule.** Do not add card shadows to the gallery, service lists, process rails, or forms; borders and tonal contrast already define their structure.

## Shapes

The dominant form language is square, measured, and cut from sheets. Buttons, fields, cards, navigation blocks, and image frames use square corners. The persistent back control alone carries a restrained 2px corner. True circles are reserved for marks with field meaning: the inspection stamp and the Field Book's lettered project indices.

Hairline borders organize the gallery table, drafting sheet, process rails, and service grids. The Field Book adds a 40px construction grid and plan-line illustrations; Project Stories replaces visible framing with clipped full-viewport imagery and translucent rectangular selectors.

### Named Rules

**The Sheet-Cut Rule.** Default to zero-radius rectangles; a rounded card would contradict the built material language.

**The Circle Has Meaning Rule.** Use circles only for stamps, indexed callouts, or other explicit site-document notation.

## Components

Shared components keep interactions familiar while allowing route-level colors and compositions to remain distinct.

### Buttons

- **Shape:** Square by default; the floating back control uses the sole restrained corner treatment.
- **Primary:** Uppercase labels, 53–58px high, warm route accent, white text, and an arrow separated by generous inline space.
- **Hover / Focus:** Actions deepen or move by 1–2px; icon arrows may translate 4px. Keyboard focus is always a visible 3px coral outline with 4px offset.
- **Route Variants:** Use Stories Orange, Field Red, or Standard Orange inside those worlds. Shared Clay belongs to the gallery, Daily Build Log, and the common estimate form.

### Chips

- **Style:** Project Stories selectors are sharp translucent night panels with a chalk hairline border, condensed uppercase labels, and a numbered prefix on desktop.
- **State:** The active selector fills with Stories Orange and turns all text white. On mobile the three selectors become an equal-width bottom rail and omit their numbers.

### Cards / Containers

- **Concept Card:** A dark framed preview above a warm paper copy panel with an accent number and square arrow control. The featured card is spatially dominant, not merely recolored.
- **Service and Process Containers:** Flat grids separated by hairline rules; internal padding varies by world but never becomes a floating rounded tile.
- **Field Book Containers:** Framed directly into the drafting sheet with visible borders and measured alignment.

### Inputs / Fields

- **Style:** Transparent, square fields with only a current-color bottom rule and 15px vertical padding.
- **Focus:** The global coral focus outline remains visible; do not remove it in favor of color change alone.
- **State:** Placeholder text uses reduced opacity. Submitted and sending states stay inside the action button, and the form always carries its demo-only note.

### Navigation

- **Shared Brand Mark:** A 31px square ruler mark followed by an uppercase wordmark.
- **Desktop:** Routes use sparse text links and one estimate action or underlined conversion link.
- **Mobile:** Secondary navigation links disappear; the brand remains, and the back control moves to a reachable upper-right overlay.

### Project Stage

Project Stories uses a single active slide, a dark directional scrim, a 550ms crossfade, and a slower image-scale settle. Inactive slides are both transparent and hidden. Reduced-motion preferences collapse these transitions to effectively instantaneous changes.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** preserve all four palette and composition variants as separate complete worlds.
- **Do** let architectural imagery carry the first impression and support it with believable, plainspoken specificity.
- **Do** use square controls, hairline rules, uppercase labels, and warm construction-derived accents.
- **Do** keep the persistent route back control and the front-end-demo disclosure on every estimate form.
- **Do** stack grids cleanly at 980px and compress to direct mobile flows at 680px.

### Don't:

- **Don't** turn the showcase into one generic photo-and-card template or apply one route's layout to all four.
- **Don't** replace the serif/condensed/sans hierarchy with a single neutral sans-serif voice.
- **Don't** add soft rounded cards, ambient dashboard shadows, glass effects, or cold technology colors.
- **Don't** invent testimonials, ratings, credentials, project metrics, or other proof the fictional contractor does not own.
- **Don't** hide keyboard focus, remove reduced-motion behavior, or imply that the demo form is already connected to a backend.
